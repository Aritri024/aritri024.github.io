Project

20 kW High-Temperature Furnace & Test Platform

Mechanical design, instrumentation, automated control, experimental diagnostics, and performance validation.

← Back to selected projects

Overview

At the University of Minnesota T.E. Murphy Engine Lab, I designed and validated a 20 kW high-temperature furnace and modular test hardware for controlled experimental studies.

Problem

The work required a repeatable high-temperature platform that could support prototype iteration, controlled ammonia/methane/air delivery, synchronized measurements, and system-performance characterization.

Approach

  • Developed SolidWorks models, GD&T drawings, tolerance stack-ups, BOMs, and DFM/DFA-based design iterations for prototypes operating near 1000 deg C.
  • Engineered and commissioned an ammonia/methane/air process system using mass flow controllers, pressure/temperature sensors, NI cDAQ, and LabVIEW.
  • Planned a four-variable Design of Experiments and used statistical analysis, FTIR, PIV, chemiluminescence, and CT measurements to define operating windows and characterize NOx/ammonia trends.
  • Applied RCA and FMEA during system development and testing.

Results

  • Delivered repeatable process data and engineering recommendations with CenterPoint Energy and U of M Duluth.
  • The resume reports a 60% reduction in NOx compared with conventional burners.
